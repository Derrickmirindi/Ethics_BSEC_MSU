01 What the numbers mean

Q3–Q15 use the supplied prototype's 1–5 scale. The item-weighted mean is the sum of numeric ratings divided by their count. The median respects response ordering; the mean additionally assumes equal spacing between scale points.

The domain means cover trust and ethics (Q3–Q7), partnership practice (Q8–Q12), and empowerment and recognition (Q13–Q15). An equal-domain comparison weights each available domain mean equally. It is shown only when all three domains have numeric responses; sparse coverage still limits interpretation.

Ratings of 1–2 trigger review prompts; 4–5 are reported as positive ratings. These are transparent, illustrative rules, not validated thresholds. Q3 concerns initial expectations and is not equivalent to a measured outcome.

02 Missingness, comparability & evidence

Blank items are unanswered. N/A is an explicit response without a numeric value. Both are excluded from means and the median, but reported separately. Numeric coverage is the number of 1–5 ratings divided by 13; assessment completion also includes answered non-rated sections and N/A.

Before comparing people, communities, or time points, agree on item applicability and a missing-data policy. Do not treat different subsets of answered questions as equivalent. Pair item distributions with consented qualitative evidence and protect small groups.

No reliability, factor structure, or predictive validity has been established by this webpage. The five domains are organizational groupings, not validated subscales.

03 Community governance & responsible use

Before field deployment, co-review wording and response anchors with community partners, use an institution-approved research platform, obtain required ethics review and consent, and document access, retention, withdrawal, and incident-handling procedures.

This educational version is for adult reflection (18+). It intentionally does not request email addresses or telephone numbers. Interview permission is a practice response, not an enrollment or contact request. No actual invitation is sent.

Agree on who interprets the findings, who owns each response action, when progress will be reviewed, and how results return to the community. Participation and acknowledgment should follow community preferences.

04 Conceptual basis & adaptation

The supplied BSEC framework adapts ideas of humility, respect and inclusion, reciprocity, preparation, safety and well-being, accountability, evaluation, and learning and reflection. These principles are described by the Stanford Haas Center's Principles of Ethical and Effective Service (2019).

The full principles and reflection questions provide further context. This page is an adaptation and educational interface, not a claim of Stanford or Morgan State approval, an official standard, or a validated instrument.

Question titles, prompts, and attributions are preserved from the supplied BSEC prototype. Expanded analytical rules and follow-through prompts are interface additions and should be reviewed before research use.
